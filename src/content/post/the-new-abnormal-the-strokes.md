Music reviews make most sense right after release, when first impressions are fresh and the album sits in the moment it came out. Coming back years later asks a different question, whether it still holds up. The New Abnormal came out in 2020, their first album in seven years, won the Grammy for Best Rock Album, and six years on I'm still listening to it. That feels worth writing about.

Ode to the Mets, The Adults Are Talking and Bad Decisions are among my most played tracks of the last few years, from any album.

What keeps bringing me back to this album is the sincerity. It's a rough diamond, deliberately unpolished. Even the cover fits. It's Jean-Michel Basquiat's 1981 painting Bird on Money, raw, layered and a little chaotic. Julian Casablancas sings about friendship, regret, growing older, missed connections and people drifting in and out of our lives. It's familiar territory, but he sings it like he means every word. The best way I can put it is that the album is nostalgic without sounding trapped in the past.

My introduction to Julian wasn't through The Strokes. It was through Instant Crush on Daft Punk's Random Access Memories, back in 2013. I loved the song but never followed through.

Then came 2020. I was in Rwanda for work, on a trip that was meant to last a month and [ended up lasting almost eight](/blog/on-melancholy-hill). The only time I had for music, or for catching up on anything online, was the ten-minute ride between the camp and the site, once in the morning and once in the evening. The rest of the day was chaos, work all day long and the uncertainty of what was happening back home, where all my friends and family were. We were reasonably safe there, because the Rwandan government and our site team had put strict measures in place to keep the virus out. In the middle of all that, The New Abnormal arrived, and Bad Decisions became the song for those rides, which felt about right, considering the circumstances. Looking back, it was the soundtrack to those strange months, and one of the few good things about them.

My favourite moment on the album comes early in Ode to the Mets, when Julian says, "Drums please, Fab."

<div class="lyrics">
Easy to say, easy to do<br>
But it's not easy, well, maybe for you<br>
Hope that you find it, hope that it's good<br>
Hope that you read it, think that you should<br>
Cuts you some slack as he sits back<br>
Sizes you up, plans his attack<br>
Ah-da-da<br>
Drums please, Fab.
</div>

As I understand it, there wasn't some elaborate plan behind it. Julian sat at a keyboard, felt something, and said those words. Fabrizio Moretti then improvised the drum part in a single take. Rick Rubin produced the album, and he's known for stripping things back rather than polishing them. There was no rehearsal and no endless revising, and you can hear it.

There's another layer to Ode to the Mets that I love. Apparently the title came from a baseball game where the New York Mets were eliminated, but the song isn't really about baseball. The Mets become a symbol for that person, dream, or ambition that never quite lived up to expectations, yet you keep rooting for anyway. 

Speaking of baseball, if you're anxious about AI and what it means for the future, watch the music video for The Adults Are Talking. Humans go up against robots in everything from boxing to cooking to baseball. The robots are faster, stronger, and better in every conceivable way, obliterating the humans completely. Then a single raindrop lands on the pitcher robot's vision sensor. The pitch hits Julian and he walks. The next batter steps up and cracks one deep. Julian rounds the bases and scores. The humans completely lose their minds over that one moment of chance. One tiny imperfection and a lot of luck is all it takes.

The funniest part is that in this future full of hyper-advanced robots, the one thing that hasn't been automated is the baseball umpire. Considering how many calls they get wrong every season, that might be the least realistic part of the whole video.

I wish I could listen to this album for the first time again, and I envy anyone who still gets to discover it. If you've already fallen for The New Abnormal, we probably have enough in common to share a beer and let Julian soundtrack the evening.

P.S. I never went back to the older Strokes albums, but I really like their latest one, Reality Awaits, which came out in July 2026. A lot of fans have given Julian a hard time for the autotune on it. I love the album anyway, especially Falling Out of Love, which has grown on me since I first heard it a couple of months ago.
