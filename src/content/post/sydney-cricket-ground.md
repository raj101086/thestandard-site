In August, when Josh told me to plan for a week in Australia, all I could think about was the visa, the flights and the wrecked sleep. Then my itinerary was finalised, I saw that my flight home was on Sunday evening, and I knew exactly what I'd be doing that morning. When Sunday came, I checked out of [the hotel in Mascot](/blog/sydney), left my luggage with the reception and went straight to the station. I'd thought about stopping at a café for breakfast on the way, but the excitement of reaching the SCG just wouldn't let me stop anywhere.

I was running behind. I'd wanted to be there before 10:30 to see the first ball bowled, and it was already 10 by the time I reached Central. From there I had to work out the rest myself: out of Central, a walk towards Haymarket, and the L2 light rail to Moore Park. It was my first tram ride in Australia, and it was a great experience. Public transport here is really amazing, and I wish that someday we'll have public transport as good as this back home, in efficiency and in quality.

Most of the people on that tram got off at Moore Park, headed where I was headed. You could see the tall light towers of the ground from far off. From the moment I stepped down and had my first look at the stadium, I was spellbound. I followed the crowd towards it, and when I reached the walls I couldn't see a ticket window anywhere, which got my anxiety going. At Gate A I asked the security officer where I could buy a ticket.

"Just go in, darling," she said. "It's free." I thanked her and walked in through the gates, and the first thing I saw was a life-size statue of Steve Waugh, one of the greatest Australian cricketers and a man who has done a lot of charity work in India, including for Udayan, a home near Kolkata for the children of leprosy patients.

I got in while the second over was underway, and a steward pointed me to a section where I could sit. I ended up in the Ladies' Pavilion, a few rows from the fence. It was a Sheffield Shield match, Queensland against New South Wales, with Queensland batting, and I was pleasantly surprised to see a few players from the national team: Nathan Lyon, the legendary spinner, and Sean Abbott in the NSW line-up, and Marnus Labuschagne, back playing for Queensland. I was finally sitting at the historic SCG.

With each ball being bowled, it slowly started coming back, and soon I was overwhelmed. I remembered the heartbreaking losses India had suffered on this ground, and the great moments it had given the team and individual players. Catching a Sydney Test meant getting up really early, because play started at 5 in the morning in India. Sometimes India played so badly that by the time we were up and had switched on the TV, we were already reeling. That was Indian cricket in the 90s, and on most tours, individual brilliance was the only saving grace. The SCG has never been kind to India or to Indian fans. India have won only one Test here, back in 1978, but over the years we went from humiliating defeats to fighting, defiant draws against the Australians.

Two knocks from here I remember most fondly. The first was VVS Laxman's swashbuckling 167 off 198 balls in January 2000, in a losing cause. The second was Sachin's 241 not out, in January 2004, the innings that showed the little man really was a cricket god. Getting out outside the off stump had become his weakness, so for the whole of that innings he didn't play the cover drive. It was a display of character and patience, and it set India up to draw the Test at the SCG. That draw meant India drew a series in Australia for the first time in more than two decades, and they spoiled the party for Steve Waugh's farewell series while doing it.

My brother and I have a name for a certain kind of place. On every trip there's one spot where you feel you've achieved something just by being there, and we call it achievement wali jagah. On the Spiti trip it was [Chandratal](/blog/spiti-part-3). In China it was [the Great Wall](/blog/finding-true-love-near-north-korea). On this trip it was the SCG.

I've been to a FIFA World Cup semi-final in [Moscow](/blog/the-russian-welcome), but somehow a domestic match at the SCG made me more emotional. A semi-final between England and Croatia in Russia carried no personal emotion for me beyond being there, while the SCG is where Indian players have given their blood and sweat, and that mattered more. And cricket was my first love. I may have fallen out of it since, but the memories are still there, a childhood full of core memories tied to cricket, and for someone who used to eat and sleep cricket, it was an emotional, surreal experience. I wasn't alone either. I could see so many Indians in the stands, most of them visiting Sydney, who had come to see this iconic stadium. One of them, a Sardarji, took this photo of me in front of the turf.

![Me in front of the turf at the Sydney Cricket Ground](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795657951/IMG_5544_qzwbth.jpg)

I stayed until the end of the first session at 12:30, about 30 overs in all, in which Queensland scored 80 to 90 runs for the loss of one wicket. I got to watch the skilful master Lyon and the deceptive quick Abbott bowl, and the tenacious Labuschagne bat. It was warm and sunny, but that didn't stop me from enjoying every minute of the play. Sitting there watching the match, I decided I should watch one at [Old Trafford](/blog/someday) too. The first thing I did when I got home and met my girlfriend was ask her to convince her father to plan a trip to the UK. He's an avid Manchester United fan as well, a much bigger one than me.

At 12:30 the players went off for lunch, and during the break Nathan Lyon came over to sign autographs for the fans. I settled for a photo.

![Nathan Lyon signing autographs at the SCG](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795657931/IMG_5541_yhr9cg.jpg)

I took a few last photos and left by 12:50, took the light rail back from Moore Park, collected my luggage from the hotel and headed for the airport and the 5:30 flight home.

I'd been to plenty of tourist places that week, but what I felt that morning at the SCG was different. It was a flood of memories from growing up, when the only religion I followed was cricket, and Sachin was its god.
