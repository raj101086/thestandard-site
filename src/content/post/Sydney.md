For a week in Newcastle, someone else had done the thinking for me. Josh drove, Greg guided, and all I had to do was say yes to milkshakes and beers by the lake. On Saturday I was on my own for the first time, with one afternoon to see Sydney before the SCG the next morning, and the day started better than I expected. My Uber driver to Newcastle Interchange was an older man who looked pretty dapper himself, and as I got out he complimented my beard. Coming from him, it meant a lot.

On paper, the trip to Sydney looked easy, and for once it was. The 9:50 left on time and reached Central at 12:30, three hours by the window. Somewhere along the way the train stopped at a station called Morisset, and I took a photo of the sign and the bench beneath it. Two days earlier Greg had shown me the [Bogey Hole](/blog/newcastle-part-2), the rock pool convicts cut for Commandant James Morisset's private bath. The town is named after the same man, who camped there in 1823 on the first overland journey from Newcastle to Sydney, the route my train was taking.

![A bench under the Morisset station sign, on the train from Newcastle to Sydney](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795675962/IMG_5400_wpxe3x.jpg)

From Central I worked out the local trains myself and found one to Mascot, near the airport, where I'd booked a room at Meriton Suites Mascot Central, a beautiful apartment on the 13th floor. [Shanks](/blog/shanks), who has ridden in my backpack for nine years, got a window to himself and a view of Sydney. I made myself an espresso with the little machine in the room, changed my clothes, and walked straight back to the station for a train to Circular Quay.

![Red-Haired Shanks at the 13th-floor window of my hotel in Mascot, Sydney](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795657938/IMG_5412_j5olfl.jpg)

It felt like a summer afternoon, and it seemed to have brought thousands of tourists to the same place as me. Circular Quay was buzzing. A violinist was playing All of Me by John Legend, and a little further along a band was playing a song I can't recall now. Right outside the station a massive cruise liner was docked, and just to the right of it was the Opera House. My picture of it came from years of cricket broadcasts and, mostly, from the 2000 Sydney Olympics, watched on television back in Bhilai. In person it was slightly underwhelming. I took a few photos anyway, and that's when I missed having a shorter lens. With only the 55-200, street photography meant walking back to fit anything into the frame, but I had to make it work, and I was happy to walk.

I headed for The Rocks Markets, which felt like a flea market, and glanced along the stalls. The Rocks is all small lanes criss-crossing up and down around Argyle Street, and I took a few of them by accident and got lost more than once. That turned out to be the theme of the day. On a trip, I doubt anyone can get lost without finding something new.

The first thing it found me was a pub with a sign calling it Sydney's oldest, the Fortune of War on George Street, so I went in to check. It was packed, but I found a small table for two in the corner, left my bag on it and went to the counter. I got a pint of Guinness, my first in many years, and I loved it. I finished it quickly, and as I stepped back outside, a man passing by told me, "Beautiful beard, baby." That was two compliments in one day, and it wasn't even 3 o'clock.

![A pint of Guinness at the Fortune of War, The Rocks](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795657942/IMG_5420_ruiulw.jpg)

The Museum of Contemporary Art was next, but the exhibition on was $35, so I skipped it and walked up to Sydney Observatory instead. After the crowds at the Quay it was peaceful up there, with a scenic view over the harbour. On my left, as I stood facing the Opera House, was a tall, twisting glass tower I didn't know the name of. It turned out to be Crown Sydney, at Barangaroo, the tallest building in the city.

![Crown Sydney at Barangaroo, the tallest building in Sydney](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795668959/IMG_5440_mpejz4.jpg)

A wedding photoshoot was going on outside, and from inside the observatory I took a photo of the photographer taking a photo of the couple. It was the kind of street shot the telephoto lens was good for after all.

![A wedding photographer at work outside Sydney Observatory, seen from inside](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795669210/DSCF9957_gpwyqx.jpg)

On the way down the stairs from the observatory, I stopped opposite the Garrison Church on Argyle Street, where another wedding was taking place. My eyes, though, were on a red door in the church, behind a hedge across the road. I was standing on the footpath framing the shot when a man walked past, stopped a few metres on and came back to stand next to me. "You've got a good shot there," he said, and took a picture of the red door with his phone as well.

![The red door of the Garrison Church, behind a hedge on Argyle Street, The Rocks](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795678466/DSCF9963_ntv7d8.jpg)

From there I walked all the way across the Sydney Harbour Bridge towards Milsons Point. The walk was a bit tiring but well worth it, because it gave me a different view of the Opera House, with boats and ships passing in front of it. From the top I got two photos I really liked. One was of a street below, its parked cars covered in jacaranda flowers. The other was of a boat tearing through the water, and of all the photos I took in Australia, that one is my favourite. I like how it cuts through the water, and the mix of white and blue gives it a symmetry of its own.

![A street of parked cars covered in jacaranda flowers, seen from the Sydney Harbour Bridge](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795677814/IMG_5452_yv6uxw.jpg)

![A boat tearing through Sydney Harbour, seen from the top of the Harbour Bridge](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795601950/DSCF9982_ddzhgs.jpg)

Just as I came down the stairs at the far end, it started to rain, and once again the day found me something. I took shelter in a gelato shop. I was hungry by then, because I hadn't had lunch and was running on nothing but breakfast in Newcastle and the Guinness, so I ordered a medium cup with two scoops, and it was yummy. When the rain stopped I walked down to the wharf at Milsons Point and took the ferry back across to Circular Quay, filming the Opera House as we came in. From the water it looked majestic. This was the building I'd seen since childhood on television and in photos, and now it was right in front of me, and I was in awe of it.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/xLh_iu3NYdk" title="Sydney Opera House from the Milsons Point ferry" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Then I walked right up to it, and close enough to touch, the awe faded. The shells are covered in more than a million tiles, and up close their texture and finish didn't look nearly as great as they do from afar or on a screen. It's still an iconic landmark, but one that looks better from a distance, from the deck of a ferry or from a sofa in front of the television. Still, I was standing in front of it, and a fellow solo traveller snapped a picture of me to prove it. I returned the favour.

![Me in front of the Sydney Opera House, with the Harbour Bridge behind](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1795657941/IMG_5484_aswhih.jpg)

From there I walked into the Royal Botanic Garden, heading for Mrs Macquarie's Chair, and with the Opera House across the water, I thought of Dil Chahta Hai, where Aamir Khan's Akash follows Preity Zinta's Shalini to Sydney. I thought of the scene at the Opera House where Shalini tells Akash what the opera singer is singing about, and before that the song between them, Jaane Kyon, which I think was shot in this same garden. After the Olympics and the cricket, that film was the other place I'd seen this view.

By 7 my legs had had enough. My Apple Health app said I'd walked almost 9.5 kilometres, and when I finally got close to the Chair, the path was closed for a music festival called Harbourlife. "Summer starts with us," said the board, which was news to me after shivering through a 15-degree evening in Newcastle. It was a bit frustrating, but the view from where I stood wasn't bad either. I took some photos, and right at the edge, where the guard stopped me going any further, a double rainbow came out over the Opera House. It had saved one last surprise for the end of the day.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/jLE6CwzwC5k" title="Sydney Opera House and Harbour Bridge from the Royal Botanic Garden" allowfullscreen></iframe>

That was also when my X-T30 gave up. It started showing an error and wouldn't take any more pictures. I found out later that it had simply run out of file numbers. It didn't bother me much, because I'd already shot a lot that day.

Hungry again by then, because gelato tastes good but doesn't fill you up, I walked back towards Circular Quay hoping for a table at the Opera Bar, but it was packed to the brim and I had no luck. So I took the train back to Mascot. Leaving the hotel that afternoon, I'd noticed quite a few Asian restaurants nearby, so I checked a few reviews on Google and settled on Great Western, which called itself an authentic Chinese restaurant. I went to see whether it really was, and boy, was it. As soon as I stepped in, I was back in 2017, in one of the restaurants in Dalian from [my time in China](/blog/chinese-hot-pot-my-last-meal). Apart from the missing lazy Susan, everything felt the same: rows and rows of baijiu, Asahi and Harbin beer, and a menu full of dishes I'd loved eating there. I was happy. I ordered a few of them, with an Asahi and a Harbin.

The full Opera Bar had sent me to China, the rain had sent me to gelato, and the wrong turns had sent me to a Guinness. It was a wonderful end to a day that had been good to me from the start, and after a hearty dinner my hotel bed was the only thing I was looking for. The next morning I had somewhere to be: the SCG.
