My first motorcycle, [the Caliber](/blog/hoodibaba), carried three and sometimes four of us to cricket tournaments, with total disregard for safety. We were just dumb. Eighteen years later, when [Mashooqa](/blog/mashooqa-royal-enfield-interceptor) arrived in May 2021, the first things I bought were a helmet and a jacket, ordered online so they'd reach me in Bhilai before I rode her to Pune. Both got a proper test on the first day. I left home at 5 in the morning, it started raining heavily near Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, and I reached the Lemon Tree there around 8, completely drenched. Five years, two motorcycles and more than 50,000 kilometres later, I've bought a fair amount of riding gear. Some of it was a hit and some of it a miss, and a couple of things from that first order are still with me.

The helmet was the Aspida Tourance, and at around ₹5,000 in 2021 it was a steal. It became my everyday helmet for the Saturday rides that followed, out towards Solapur, Tamhini, Mahabaleshwar, Nashik and Lonavala. It has a short tinted inner visor for when the sun is in your eyes. The noise isolation isn't great, and the strap doesn't have a double D-ring, which is usually the gold standard for locking a helmet. Other than that it's a solid helmet, and I still have it.

The jacket was the DSG, around ₹10,000, in black and red. It looked awesome, and maybe that's why I bought it. I felt safe riding in it, but it was extremely heavy, the ventilation was poor, and by the end of those Saturday rides I used to be completely drained. It just broke my back. I still have it too, and it comes out two or three times a year, when the jacket I actually use is in the wash.

A few months later, in a Royal Enfield apparel sale, I bought the RE boots for around ₹4,000. They look cool, I have to say that: tall black canvas, a solid toe guard and leather protection over the ankle. They were also heavy, like the Red Chief and Woodland boots before them, and every step felt like an effort. In November 2023 I bought the [Birkenstock Martons](/blog/birkenstock-marton) for a work trip to China, and once I started wearing them on the Interceptor, the RE boots just gathered dust. These days I wear them as casual shoes and not for riding.

In 2022 we made our first attempt at Zanskar. We tried and failed, and just before it I'd bought a pair of Scala short gloves for ₹1,500 to ₹2,000. They weren't waterproof, and getting wet over and over is what finished them in the end. They were great gloves, and I only replaced them because they'd worn out so badly.

In July 2024 I bought [Snowpiercer](/blog/snowpiercer-two-years-triumph-street-triple-r), the Triumph Street Triple 765 R, and the second round of buying started at the showroom. Triumph had an offer on, so I got the Braddan mesh jacket at a discount along with the bike, and I don't remember exactly what I paid. It's black mesh with quilted shoulders and cream and red stripes down the sleeves. Where the DSG trapped the heat, the mesh keeps the air moving, and it's perfect for the city and for most places in India. The only time I feel the pinch is on chilly winter mornings, riding out early to [Tamhini](/blog/tamhini_1) or Mahabaleshwar, when I shiver inside it.

The first thing I went out and bought after that was the KYT R2R Pro, for ₹16,000. It's a fantastic helmet, stable at high speeds, and it doesn't wobble around. At about 1.6 kilograms it's slightly heavy compared to the newer carbon fibre helmets, and when my girlfriend was on holiday in Phuket I asked her to look at Shoei and Arai helmets for me. She didn't find any good ones, and the KYT is still the one I wear.

I fitted it with a Bluarmor C30, which cost ₹9,500, and since then most of my rides have been filled with music and Google Maps directions. It earned its place on the Diwali ride home, the 1,050 kilometres I did in a single day to surprise my parents. It was connected to my phone for close to 13 hours, playing music and navigating the whole way, and it still had 30% battery left at the end. I did that ride again this November. My other plans fell apart, and late one night I decided to ride home. I left Pune at 4:30 the next morning and reached Durg at 6:30 in the evening, 1,010 kilometres later, and the C30 and my iPhone, running navigation, were both on for all 14 hours and both lasted the distance. The JBL speakers sound good too. The one problem is the buttons. They're so difficult to press with gloves on that I struggle to operate it while riding, which defeats the purpose of the thing.

![Me on Snowpiercer in the KYT R2R Pro with the Bluarmor C30 fitted, and the Triumph Braddan mesh jacket](https://res.cloudinary.com/dpsbm1jkq/image/upload/v1794289605/IMG_2453_v6tudv.jpg)

For boots I went to Gear & Throttle and paid ₹18,000 for a pair of Shimas. I didn't want full-length racing boots, because I wanted something I could also wear for a casual evening. In the city I mostly ride in my [Chuck Taylor waterproof sneakers](/blog/chuck-taylor), and the Shimas are for the weekend long rides. They've been through everything the Triumph has been through: gravel, potholes, heavy rain and shallow streams, and nothing has broken so far. Once I turn the knobs and the laces tighten, my feet feel safe and secure.

The gloves I ride in now are Ixon short racing gloves, which cost ₹8,000 at Riese Moto, next to the Kawasaki showroom in Shivajinagar. I've had them for close to two years and they've been fantastic. Even in the rain they've held their own, which the Scalas never did. The leather needed some breaking in, but once that was done they became perfect gloves. Ixon makes a full-gauntlet version as well, and I'll definitely be looking at more of their gear.

The Triumph gloves were the other miss of this round, the leather ones with the Union Jack on them. They look good, but my fingers don't feel dexterous inside them, the clutch and brake are difficult to operate, and there's no knuckle or palm protection in case of a slide. They're perfect for a cafe ride on a classic like the Bonneville or Mashooqa. Not that I always dress for those. On [one forced holiday](/blog/mashooqas-day-out) this year I rode Mashooqa in a T-shirt, jeans and the Martons, with no helmet, no gloves and no riding boots, which was neither safe nor sane. The Bluarmor was on the helmet I'd left at home, so I didn't have navigation either.

Looking at the list now, the misses have something in common. The DSG looked awesome, the RE boots look cool and the Union Jack gloves look good, and in all three cases the look was the best thing about them.

For two years I've ridden the Triumph mostly solo, and the gear I bought suited that: a mesh jacket, short gloves, and boots I could wear out in the evening. Now I'm looking for company, and riding groups usually have a strict rule about complete riding gear. So the next things on the list are a pair of riding pants, which I've never owned, and proper racing boots, either the Alpinestars Supertech or a pair from TCX. More than twenty years after riding three to a bike on the Caliber to cricket tournaments, I'm shopping for riding pants so that a group will let me ride with them.
