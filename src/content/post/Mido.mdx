For almost three years, the Mido Ocean Star Tribute Gradient has been on my wrist nearly every day, and it wasn't even the plan.

Mido wasn't sold in India then, so I asked a friend visiting Dubai to pick one up for me. The original choice was the 38mm [Hamilton Khaki Field Murph](/blog/longines-avigation-bigeye), the one from Interstellar, with its clean dial and a story behind it. It was sold out, so the Mido was the backup. Looking back, it was the right call.

My friend and his wife lived in the same society as me, so the day after they got back, he came over with the bag. The watch came in a big box, which is still somewhere in my almirah, and in person it looked much better than in the photos on Mido's website. Mine has a grey gradient dial with an orange seconds hand, an odd combination that works far better than it should. I've never changed the strap either. It came on rubber, and unlike the rubber strap on my Tissot Seastar, which I swapped for leather, this one I actually like.

The 40.5mm case sits well on my wrist without trying too hard. It manages to feel vintage and modern at the same time, the gradient dial shifts with the light, and the proportions just work. I've worn it on long motorcycle rides, slow weekends and entirely forgettable Tuesday mornings, and it has never felt out of place.

I also own a Tissot Seastar Powermatic 80, and I like its weight more. It's chunkier, and I can feel it on my wrist. In every other way I prefer the Mido: how it sits, the black strap against the grey dial, that dash of orange. It isn't perfect. The lume is weak, and the rotating bezel is much stiffer to turn than the Seastar's. Even so, when I reach for a diver, it's almost always the Mido.
